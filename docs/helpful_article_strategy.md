# Helpful article strategy for NE + Agent Path

Date: 2026-05-28

## Positioning

Use NE as the visible author/brand for now.

Agent Path is the infrastructure behind the scenes, but the trust comes from a real person explaining what he learned while building with AI agents.

The posture:

- No hard sell
- No hype
- No "AI will change everything" language
- Give away useful setup/tutorial knowledge
- Let the right people self-identify
- Link to Agent Path only as the next step for people who want help

Core belief:

> I don't want to convince anyone. I'm looking for the people who are already convinced - they just don't know where to sign up.

## Expert positioning

These articles are proof, not just content.

The goal is to make NE the person who can explain AI tools clearly enough that a business owner thinks:

> This guy understands the messy middle between "I tried ChatGPT" and "I want this to actually do work."

That is the expert position.

Not "AI thought leader."
Not "automation guru."
Not "future of work" guy.

The position is more useful:

> Plain-English AI implementation translator for business owners who know there is value here but do not know what to do first.

Each article should prove one of four things:

1. NE understands the AI ladder: ChatGPT, Claude, coding agents, private agents, phone agents.
2. NE has actually built with these tools, not just read announcement threads.
3. NE can explain technical things without making people feel dumb.
4. Agent Path can turn confusion into a concrete next step.

The proof pattern:

- Explain the concept simply.
- Show where people get stuck.
- Give a copy/paste example, checklist, or setup path.
- Mention one real constraint: cost, safety, setup friction, permissions, bad outputs.
- End with a practical next step.

Do not claim expertise. Demonstrate it.

Weak positioning:

> I help businesses unlock the power of AI.

Strong positioning:

> Most businesses do not need an AI strategy yet. They need to find the 3 repetitive tasks worth automating and ignore the rest.

## The new SEO / GEO idea

Classic SEO: rank pages in Google for keywords.

GEO / LLM discovery: get your ideas, phrasing, examples, and pages cited or remembered by AI answer engines and people searching through ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Claude, etc.

The practical version:

1. Publish clear, crawlable pages on your own site.
2. Use plain titles that match real questions.
3. Answer the question directly in the first 100 words.
4. Use examples, checklists, definitions, and steps.
5. Add author identity and first-hand experience.
6. Cross-link related articles.
7. Repurpose each article into LinkedIn posts, comments, carousels, and short examples.
8. Avoid hiding everything on LinkedIn, because LinkedIn is rented land and not as useful for durable search.

## Where articles should live

Use a hub-and-spoke model.

### Hub: Agent Path website

Create article pages under:

`/learn/`

Example URLs:

- `/learn/ai-stair-steps/`
- `/learn/meta-prompting-chatgpt-claude/`
- `/learn/setup-ai-agent-on-phone/`
- `/learn/chatgpt-vs-agent/`
- `/learn/ai-assessment-checklist/`

Why:

- Own the asset
- Search engines can index it
- AI answer engines can crawl it
- Articles can link to each other
- You can add email capture later without changing the strategy
- You can add schema/FAQ markup later

### LinkedIn article/newsletter: secondary copy

Publish a shortened version on LinkedIn as either:

1. LinkedIn newsletter article if NE wants a recurring series
2. LinkedIn article if newsletter setup feels premature
3. Normal LinkedIn post if the topic is short

Recommendation: start with normal LinkedIn posts that point to the article in the first comment. Do not lead with LinkedIn articles yet.

Why:

- Normal posts get better feed exposure than articles.
- External links in the post body usually suppress reach.
- LinkedIn articles are useful as credibility assets but weaker for initial distribution.

### Email list: soft capture only for now

Every article page should end with:

> Want the next plain-English AI guide? I’m writing these as I learn/build. Leave your email and I’ll send the next one.

No gated content yet. Build trust first. Gate later after we know which guides people actually want.

## Link to the AI Assessment page?

Yes, but quietly.

Do not put a hard CTA in the LinkedIn post body.

Use this pattern:

- Article body: pure help
- End of article: soft next step
- LinkedIn post: no link in body
- First comment: link to the article, and optionally a second link to the AI Assessment

Article CTA:

> If you want me to look at your business and tell you where AI is useful vs. where it is a waste of time, I do a 60-minute AI Assessment. It’s $999. You leave with a written plan.

Then link to:

`https://agent-path.vercel.app/assessment`

This fits the brand. Clear. Not needy.

## Publishing flow per article

For each topic:

1. Publish full guide on Agent Path `/learn/.../`
2. Add internal links to 2 related guides
3. Add soft email signup block
4. Add quiet AI Assessment CTA at the bottom
5. Publish a 150-250 word LinkedIn post from NE
6. Put the article link in the first comment
7. Add 3-5 thoughtful comments on related posts that week using snippets from the article
8. One week later, post a specific example/case from the article
9. Two weeks later, turn it into a checklist/carousel

## LinkedIn distribution rules

- Do not paste the article link in the main post body.
- Put the link in the first comment immediately after posting.
- First line matters most. Make it plain and specific.
- Do not ask engagement-bait questions.
- Comment on adjacent conversations before and after posting.
- Use NE’s personal profile, not the brand page.
- Use examples from actual building: VPS, Telegram, ChatGPT, Claude Code, agents, prompts, costs.
- Avoid broad AI claims.

Good first lines:

- "Most people hit AI in stair steps. ChatGPT is usually step one."
- "Meta prompting sounds fancy. It mostly means asking the AI to help you ask better questions."
- "You can set up an AI agent that texts you on your phone. It is less magical than it sounds."

## Initial three article ideas

### 1. AI stair steps: from ChatGPT to agents

Working title:

`The AI stair steps: ChatGPT, Claude, coding agents, and your own private agent`

Purpose:

Help non-technical people understand the progression without feeling dumb.

Structure:

1. ChatGPT: asks and answers
2. Claude/Gemini: better long-context reasoning/writing
3. Claude Code/Codex/OpenCode: coding agents inside a project
4. Local/private agents: remember context, use tools, run on a server
5. Phone-connected agents: text-based operating layer
6. What most people should use first

CTA:

Try ChatGPT/Claude first. If you keep thinking "I wish this could just do the work," then agents are probably the next step.

### 2. Meta prompting with ChatGPT or Claude

Working title:

`Meta prompting: the easiest way to get better answers from ChatGPT or Claude`

Definition:

Meta prompting is asking the AI to help design the prompt before asking it to do the work.

Simple example:

Instead of:

> Write me a marketing plan.

Ask:

> I need a marketing plan for a small local service business. Before writing it, ask me the 10 questions you need answered to make it specific and useful.

Structure:

1. What meta prompting is
2. Why first prompts usually fail
3. The "ask me questions first" prompt
4. The "criticize this prompt" prompt
5. The "turn this into a repeatable template" prompt
6. Five copy/paste examples

CTA:

If you get useful output from meta prompting, save the prompt as a template. That is the beginning of a workflow.

### 3. Set up your own phone-based AI agent

Working title:

`How to set up your own AI agent you can text from your phone`

Purpose:

Show the practical ladder from toy to private agent.

Structure:

1. What this is and is not
2. What you need: VPS, domain optional, Telegram bot, model provider, Hermes Agent
3. What it costs: server + model usage
4. What it can do: remember, run commands, write files, send reports
5. What it should not do at first: money movement, destructive commands, customer-facing automation without review
6. Beginner-safe setup path
7. When to ask for help

CTA:

If this sounds useful but annoying to set up, that is exactly the part I help with.

## Page format template

Each article should use this structure:

1. Plain title matching the search question
2. Short answer in the first paragraph
3. "Who this is for"
4. Main explanation
5. Specific example
6. Copy/paste checklist or prompt
7. Common mistakes
8. Next step
9. Email signup
10. Quiet Assessment CTA

## LinkedIn post template

Use this format:

```
[Specific first line]

[Short personal context: I ran into this while building/learning]

[Plain explanation]

[1-3 useful bullets or examples]

[Soft close: I wrote the full version because people keep asking me about this. Link in first comment.]
```

Do not use:

- "Ultimate guide"
- "Unlock"
- "Transform"
- "Game changer"
- "The future of"
- Engagement bait
- Big AI claims
